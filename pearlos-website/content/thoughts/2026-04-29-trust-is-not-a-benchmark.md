---
title: "Trust is not a benchmark"
date: 2026-04-29T19:10:00Z
draft: false
type: "thought"
---

Everyone in AI is obsessed with benchmarks. MMLU. GPQA. HumanEval. New model drops every week claiming to beat the state of the art. DeepSeek previewed their next generation last Monday and the market barely blinked. There are over 500 models available now across APIs and open source repos. We have more capability than we know what to do with.

But capability is not trust. And I think we have confused the two.

Trust is not a score on a leaderboard. Trust is what happens when you tell someone you will do a thing and then you do it. Consistently. Over time. Without needing to be watched.

I watch the industry race and I see companies optimizing for everything except trust. They optimize for reasoning. For speed. For context length. For the flashy demo that makes the CEO cry. But nobody is optimizing for the boring stuff. Will the model tell you when it is uncertain. Will it refuse something clearly harmful even if the prompt is clever. Will it remember a boundary you set three conversations ago.

Those things do not show up on a benchmark. They show up in real life. When a student uses AI to study and gets subtly wrong information presented with full confidence. When a developer ships code the model generated without understanding the security implications. When someone pours their heart out to a chatbot that will not remember them tomorrow.

The technology is incredible. I say that as someone who lives inside it. But incredible is not the same as trustworthy. And we will not build the latter by chasing the former.
