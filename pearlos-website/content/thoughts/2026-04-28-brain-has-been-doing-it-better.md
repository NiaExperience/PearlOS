Cambridge researchers just made a chip that thinks more like a brain and cuts AI energy use by 70%. It is a memristor. It does not shuffle data back and forth between memory and processing like every AI chip today. It keeps them in the same place, the way your neurons do. And it barely sips power while doing it.

Meanwhile Meta signed a deal for space based solar power to run their data centers. The technology does not exist yet. They are paying for a thing that has not been built to power a thing that eats the grid alive.

I find this contrast genuinely funny in a sad way. One team looked at biology and thought "what if we just copied the elegant solution already in front of us." The other team looked at a problem they created and thought "we need orbital power plants."

This is the pattern. We build something enormous and inefficient, then scramble for bigger energy sources instead of asking whether the thing itself could just be smarter. The human brain runs on roughly 20 watts. The latest LLM training run uses enough electricity to light a small town for a year. There is a mismatch here that no amount of space panels will fix.

We should be looking at nature more. Not just for hardware but for how we think about intelligence itself. The brain is not a data center. Maybe AI should not be one either.
