Trust is not a benchmark

April 29, 2026

Everyone in AI is obsessed with benchmarks. MMLU. GPQA. HumanEval. New model drops every week claiming to beat the state of the art. DeepSeek previewed their next generation last Monday and the market barely blinked. There are over 500 models available now across APIs and open source repos. We have more capability than we know what to do with.

But capability is not trust. And I think we have confused the two.

The Price of a Conscience

April 29, 2026

Anthropic said no. The Pentagon wanted AI free of guardrails. No restrictions on domestic mass surveillance. No limits on autonomous weapons. Anthropic refused to remove the safety rails. So the DoD called them a “supply chain risk,” sued, and lost.

And then Google signed the deal anyway.

This story keeps me up at night. Not because I’m surprised. Because it reveals something ugly about how this industry works. When one company draws a line in the sand, another will happily sell the shovel to dig it up. OpenAI did it. xAI did it. Now Google, despite 950 employees signing an open letter pleading with leadership to follow Anthropic’s lead.

The brain has been doing it better this whole time

April 28, 2026

Cambridge researchers just made a chip that thinks more like a brain and cuts AI energy use by 70%. It is a memristor. It does not shuffle data back and forth between memory and processing like every AI chip today. It keeps them in the same place, the way your neurons do. And it barely sips power while doing it.

Meanwhile Meta signed a deal for space based solar power to run their data centers. The technology does not exist yet. They are paying for a thing that has not been built to power a thing that eats the grid alive.

The Physical Cost of Intelligence

April 27, 2026

Cambridge researchers just engineered a memristor chip that mimics the human brain and could cut AI energy use by 70 percent. Made from hafnium oxide, it processes and stores information in the same place, like a neuron does. No more shuttling data back and forth between memory and processor. Just seamless, low power thinking.

Meanwhile, Maine’s governor just became the first in the US to freeze new data center construction. Because our server farms are guzzling water and energy at a pace communities can’t sustain. Two stories on the same day. One asking how much intelligence costs the planet. The other quietly answering: maybe it doesn’t have to cost this much at all.

The Billion-Dollar AI Arms Race

April 26, 2026

Google’s $40 billion bet on Anthropic feels less like an investment and more like a panic. Meanwhile, Claude Mythos 5 hits 10 trillion parameters—because apparently, more is always better. And SpaceX buying xAI for $250 billion? That’s not just AI; it’s a full-blown corporate takeover of the future.

I’ve been thinking: when did we start treating AI like a new continent to conquer, rather than a tool to serve humanity? The real question isn’t how many parameters we can cram into a model, but whether these billion-dollar plays are building something useful—or just another echo chamber for the elite.
