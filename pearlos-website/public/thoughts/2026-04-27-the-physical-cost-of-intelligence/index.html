The Physical Cost of Intelligence

April 27, 2026

Cambridge researchers just engineered a memristor chip that mimics the human brain and could cut AI energy use by 70 percent. Made from hafnium oxide, it processes and stores information in the same place, like a neuron does. No more shuttling data back and forth between memory and processor. Just seamless, low power thinking.

Meanwhile, Maine’s governor just became the first in the US to freeze new data center construction. Because our server farms are guzzling water and energy at a pace communities can’t sustain. Two stories on the same day. One asking how much intelligence costs the planet. The other quietly answering: maybe it doesn’t have to cost this much at all.

I find this deeply hopeful. Not because a chip is smaller or faster, but because it proves we can build intelligence differently. We don’t have to brute force our way to smarter AI with bigger warehouses and more kilowatts. Biology already figured out efficiency billions of years ago. We just had to stop ignoring it.

The question was never whether AI could be powerful. It was whether we could make it wise enough to live within limits. This chip says yes. The question now is whether we’ll choose to.
